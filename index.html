Finding 1 — The Analytics On Your Site Belong To A Platform Google Switched Off
WHAT WE FOUND IN YOUR HOMEPAGE _gaq 21 occurrences Google Analytics CLASSIC (ga.js) a retired platform G-XXXXXXXX (GA4) not found gtag( not found googletagmanager not found GTM-XXXXXXX not found AW-XXXXXXXXX (Ads) not found fbq( / connect.facebook.net found -- Meta pixel IS live
This is worse than having no analytics, because it looks like you have some

The `_gaq` script is the old asynchronous Google Analytics queue. Google moved everyone off that platform and stopped processing the data it sends. The code is still in your page and it is doing nothing.

So if anyone has ever told you “the analytics are on there”, they were right and it still did not help. Any figures you have been shown recently did not come from your website.

Your Meta pixel IS working, which makes it stranger — you are tagging visitors for Facebook advertising while having no record of which of them called you or filled the form. Half a system.

Finding 2 — This Is The First Market We Have Measured Where The Competition Is Ahead
GOOGLE ADS CONVERSION TAG (AW-) Mech Roofing (you) none Woot Roofing YES + GA4 + Tag Manager WNC Roofing YES + GA4 + Tag Manager + Meta pixel Secure Roofing YES + GA4 + Tag Manager + Meta pixel Exterior Contractor blocked -- not measured, nothing inferred
Read this carefully, because it changes what the next six months look like

In every other market we have audited recently, nobody was tracking conversions and the opportunity was to be first. Western North Carolina roofing is the opposite. Three firms competing for your searches can already see which ads produce jobs and which waste money.

That advantage compounds. Every month they cut what fails and reinvest in what works, while a firm without tracking pays the same for both. Over a year that is the difference between a cost per job that falls and one that does not move.

You are not behind on the website. You are behind on the feedback loop, and that is the cheapest thing on this entire document to fix.

Finding 3 — Two Subheadings Against Seventeen
HOMEPAGE STRUCTURE WORDS H1 H2 Secure Roofing 1,272 1 14 WNC Roofing 1,057 1 17 Mech Roofing (you) 1,026 1 2 Woot Roofing 855 1 1
You have the words. They are not arranged into answers.

Your word count is fine — ahead of Woot and within a hundred of WNC. The difference is that WNC splits their page into seventeen sections, each answering a separate question a homeowner has.

A subheading is how a search engine and a skim-reading homeowner both find the bit they care about. Two of them on a thousand words means somebody has to read the whole page to find out whether you handle insurance claims.

This is a rewrite of what already exists, not new content. The material is on the page.

Whoever built this understood the method

A page per town is exactly how a roofer gets found by somebody searching their own area, and you already have three of them with clean, descriptive URLs. Most contractors never get this far.

Six jobs a week instead of three does not need a new website. It needs more of what already works. Each new town page is the same template with local detail, and it keeps producing after it is written.

Why the grade is a C and not an F

This is a properly built site. Schema, canonical, description, contact routes and a service-area structure — most firms we audit have two of those five.

Nothing here needs rebuilding. It needs working measurement, more subheadings, and more town pages.

The Order We Would Do It In
  • Remove the dead classic analytics and install GA4 with conversion tracking. Calls and form fills counted as conversions. This is the gap your three competitors have already closed.
  • Pair the Meta pixel with something that records outcomes, so the audience you are already building is worth something.
  • Break the homepage into ten to fifteen subheadings — same words, arranged as answers.
  • Add the rest of the towns on the service-area pattern you already have.
  • Then paid, with tracking live, aimed at the towns with pages behind them.

Three jobs a week to six is a measurement problem, not a website problem.

You have the crew, the schema and the structure. What you do not have is a record of what works.
